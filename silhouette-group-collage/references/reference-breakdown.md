# Tested group-photo patterns

## Running group in a landscape

- Preserve the exact person count before writing the prompt.
- Keep foreground people large and distant people small.
- Preserve lifted legs, raised arms, scarves, white outfits, joined hands, and carried objects.
- Use separate related silhouettes when people are spatially dispersed.
- Derive the seam phrase from the action and environment, such as `we ran toward the sun`.

## Running group at the sea

- Preserve wet-sand reflections, wave texture, windblown hair, motion blur, and sunset colour.
- Count small background people as carefully as foreground people.
- Keep joined hands connected and carried shoes attached to the correct person.
- Use a warm paper colour against cool water, then add restrained cream stars.
- A concise phrase such as `we ran to the sea` works because it describes visible action without inventing a place name.

## Horizontal group portrait

- Preserve the exact left-to-right order and all raised-arm gestures.
- Use one loose paper-doll chain while retaining count-defining gaps and clothing lengths.
- Treat a thrown snowball or another visible airborne object as a separate companion mask.
- Preserve horizontal environmental bands such as mountain, lake, shore, and foreground rocks.
- Use a wider seam phrase such as `together under winter skies` to bind the group.

## Lessons from failed drafts

- A correct reciprocal mask is insufficient when the source grain, weather, exposure, and ground texture have been smoothed away.
- Three or four tiny decorations can make the page feel unfinished; use 8–12 restrained stars across both panels.
- A group composition needs a seam phrase unless the user declines text.
- Never report a person count without checking the image carefully from left to right.
- Do not merge separated people, enlarge distant people, omit background people, break joined hands, or detach held objects.

## Core versus supporting features

Core:

- exact group preservation;
- the same photograph across two stacked panels;
- opaque silhouettes changing into matching photographic apertures;
- large tactile paper field;
- preserved source-photo texture.

Supporting but expected:

- one scene-matched handwritten seam phrase;
- 8–12 sparse handmade stars;
- a paper colour chosen to contrast with the source scene.
